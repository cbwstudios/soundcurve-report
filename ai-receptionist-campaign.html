1. Who receives it

Small, deliberately. These are people who already pay you and already trust you.

Your 225 active customers — and nobody else

Three groups come off the list before a single email goes out: anyone with no valid email address, anyone already using the AI Receptionist, and internal or test records.

Pitching a paid product to someone who already pays for it is the one mistake that damages a relationship rather than just wasting an email. There is currently no way to tell who has it — creating that flag is the first thing on the pre-send list.

Not the 6,467 old leads

The migration brought across thousands of old prospect records, some untouched for six years. Emailing them would land the whole campaign in spam folders — including the 225 that matter.

If you want to reawaken that list it is worth doing, but as its own project with a proper warm-up. Mixing it into this campaign would sink both.

2. Email only, for now — and why

Your toll-free number is sitting in the carrier approval queue for text messaging, and nothing can send by text until that clears — which can take days or weeks, with no predictable date. Building text messages into this campaign would either fail quietly or hold the whole thing hostage to an approval nobody controls.

Email is unaffected. We launch on email, and add text follow-up as a second version once the registration comes through.

What happens when someone reacts

  • They call the demo line → the emails stop, and your rep is notified with the call recording attached.
  • They click but don't call within 3 days → one short nudge, then back into the sequence.
  • They reply → the emails stop. A reply is a real conversation; an automated follow-up two days later reads badly.
  • Day 21, no click at all → they drop onto a call list for your rep.
At 225 people, that final phone follow-up is realistic — and it is probably where most of the revenue actually comes from. Treat this as a sales campaign with an email opener, not an email campaign.

5. How we know it worked

Every email ends the same way: call the demo line. A phone call is the easiest conversion to lose track of, so the tracking is designed deliberately.

A phone number used for nothing else

One number, created for this campaign, forwarding to the AI demo. It appears nowhere else — not on the website, not in ads, not in email signatures. So every single call it receives came from these emails. That one decision does most of the work.

A tracked button, not a plain phone link

The call button goes to a one-screen page carrying the number, the offer, and a callback form for people who won't dial from an email. Clicking it records who clicked, opens their opportunity, and starts the three-day nudge timer.

Calls matched back to the customer

An incoming call from a number already on file attaches itself to that customer's record. Combined with the dedicated number, you get a per-person history: opened Tuesday, clicked Thursday, called Friday, talked for four minutes.

One pipeline that is the report

A five-stage pipeline — Emailed, Clicked, Demoed, Quoted, Closed. Everyone enters at Emailed when the campaign sends. Nobody has to assemble a report; the pipeline is the report.

Weekly we report: sent, delivered, opened, clicked, called, minutes on the demo line, quoted, closed. We will show you opens but we will not make decisions on them — Apple Mail marks messages as opened whether or not anyone read them, which inflates that number for roughly half a business list. Clicks and calls are the honest measures.

6. One thing we recommend against

Automatically calling the customer when they click

This came up and we think it should stay off the table. An unrequested outbound call to a paying customer is a robocall regardless of intent, we do not hold consent on file for it, and it arrives while they are reading an email rather than when they are ready to talk.

The demo line inverts that: they call, on their own schedule — and the act of dialling is itself the strongest possible signal that they are interested.

7. What has to happen before it can send

The first two are hard blockers. Nothing sends until they are done.
  1. A way to identify customers who already have the AI Receptionist, populated from billing, so they are excluded.
  2. Email sending domain verified so messages authenticate properly and land in inboxes rather than spam, from an address your customers recognise.
  3. The demo tracking number provisioned and tested end to end — dial it, confirm the AI answers, confirm the call lands on the right customer record.
  4. Test and internal records removed from the list.
  5. The offer deadline decided — it appears in the last two emails and drives the close.
