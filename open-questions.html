1. Contract wording and website claims

Email sent September 11; you answered the first round the same day. The follow-up went out September 14 and is what remains open.

What you already settled (thank you)

  • “No Contracts” becomes “No commitments”. The 60-day notice is never enforced and comes out of the Terms.
  • Call AI is a separate product from the AI Receptionist; the pricing row stays.
  • A BAA is issued on request; a HIPAA-compliant AI Receptionist is available and custom quoted.
  • Remove SOC 2 and ISO 27001 from the site. The old uptime monitor was dropped.

3. Billing sync: questions for SoundCurve

These are about your own process and priorities.
5. How accounts get closed.
When a client leaves, does your team set the customer Inactive, use Close Account, or both? The sync treats “not Active” as inactive; we want to be sure that is the flag your team actually changes.
6. One habit at signup.
When a lead becomes a client, whoever creates the customer in VoIPBills pastes that customer ID into the company’s Origin ID field in the CRM. Ten seconds, and it makes the link between the two systems certain. Can that become part of the signup steps? (Item 7 would make this unnecessary.)
7. A signup handoff, if you want it.
At conversion, the CRM creates a starter customer in VoIPBills with the basics, set to Inactive, records the new ID, and tasks your sales team to finish the account in VoIPBills. Your team keeps full control of billing setup; the retyping goes away. Depends on Sangoma’s answers to 3 and 4. Is this something you want scoped once we hear back?
8. Services per account.
We can already read every service line on every account (plan, seats, texting, caller ID name, e911, voicemail, numbers). Carrying a summary into the CRM lets you build lists like “clients without texting” or “clients without the AI Receptionist” for targeted offers. Which services would you most want to market against?
