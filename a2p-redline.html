Text-Messaging Compliance — Review & Approval

US carriers require every business to be registered before it can send business text messages, and they review your website as part of that registration. Below is every wording change made to meet those requirements, shown as before and after. Prepared September 1, 2026.
⚑ Nothing here is live yet.
Every change below is built and verified on the staging site only. The live site is untouched. Once you approve, the changes are published to soundcurve.com and the carrier registrations are submitted.
✓ How to review
Read each before/after below. If you are happy, reply “approved” and we publish. If anything needs changing — wording, tone, a detail that is wrong — tell us which item and what to change, and we will revise it on staging and send this page back updated.

1. Privacy Policy — the change carriers check first

Carriers reject registrations whose privacy policy allows contact data to be shared with third parties for those parties' own marketing. Our previous wording matched that rejection pattern almost exactly.

Section 3 — sharing with business partners

Privacy Policy · Section 3
Before Information could be provided to “business partners” who might contact you about their own products, services, or promotions.
After Rewritten to describe what actually happens: your name and email reach a third-party platform only when that is required to set up a product you have already purchased from us. Immediately followed by the carrier-required commitment that text-messaging consent and phone numbers are never sold or shared with third parties for their own marketing.
We rewrote this section rather than deleting it, so the policy still accurately describes how bundled products get provisioned.

Section 13 — SMS messaging

Privacy Policy · Section 13
Before Described opt-in as verbal consent only, and did not cover marketing messages.
After Rewritten in full: opt-in now describes the website checkboxes visitors actually tick, the message types now include marketing, and the non-sharing commitment is repeated here.

Contact details and address

Privacy Policy · contact block
Before admin@soundcurve.com · a Covina, CA address that did not match your registration records.
After help@soundcurve.com · SouthPoint Communications, LLC DBA SoundCurve, 2477 NW Crimson Ct, McMinnville, OR 97128.

2. Terms of Service — contained a statement that would have failed on its own

The SMS section actively contradicted the registration being applied for, and the page still carried template text from an unrelated business.

Section 25 — SMS messaging

Terms of Service · Section 25
Before “We do not send marketing, promotional, or mass SMS messages.” The section also referred to “your specific printing needs” — leftover boilerplate from a print-shop template.
After Replaced with seven short clauses covering everything carriers require: who is sending, how you opt in, how to stop (STOP) and get help (HELP) with the toll-free number and support email named, carrier liability, message frequency and rates, and a link to the Privacy Policy.
Anchored so the forms can link straight to the SMS terms rather than the top of a long page.

Legal business name

Terms of Service · throughout, plus the page title
Before “South Point Communications” — two words, appearing 202 times.
After “SouthPoint Communications, LLC, doing business as SoundCurve” — one word, matching your EIN record exactly. Carriers match this against official records, so the spelling has to agree.

3. Website forms — all three

Consent has to be asked for clearly, separately for marketing and service messages, and it can never be a condition of submitting the form.

The phone-number note

Contact form and Get Started form
Before “If you provide your phone number, you are consenting to receive voice calls only, not SMS.”
After Removed. It was an explicit written refusal of SMS consent sitting on the very pages carriers review.

Consent checkboxes

All three forms
Before One combined consent box. On the Reseller form it was required — you could not submit without agreeing, which is itself a rejection reason.
After Two separate checkboxes — one for account and service messages, one for marketing messages. Both start unticked and both are optional; the form submits either way. Privacy Policy and Terms links sit directly beneath them.
The phone field on the Get Started form stays required — that is allowed. Only the consent boxes must be optional.

4. Footer — connecting the two business names

Copyright line

Site footer and landing-page footer
Before © 2026 SoundCurve, All Rights Reserved
After © 2026 SouthPoint Communications, LLC DBA SoundCurve. All Rights Reserved
Carriers need to be able to find the link between the registered company and the brand name without digging — the footer is where they look.

5. Form layout — a compliance fix, not just cosmetic

Consent text was below the fold

Get Started panel · desktop and mobile
Before On a standard laptop screen the submit button sat 167px below the visible area and the Privacy/Terms links 105px below. On phones the chat panel appeared above the form.
After Spacing tightened so the whole form — including both consent boxes and both policy links — fits on screen without scrolling, and the form now comes before the chat panel on mobile.
Registration requires a screenshot showing the checkboxes and the policy links together in one frame, and the rules state legal links must not be obscured by other elements or pop-ups.

What happens after you approve

  • These changes are published from staging to the live site.
  • Opt-in screenshots are captured from the live site (carriers re-check the real URL, so they cannot come from staging).
  • Both registrations are submitted — the toll-free number for messaging, and the local numbers separately.
  • Approval takes anywhere from a couple of days to several weeks. No business texts can be sent until it clears.
